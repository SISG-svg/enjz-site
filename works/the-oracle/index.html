Hors Série

انصهري بلورةً سحريةً لا تنطفئ متى بردت

Melt into a magic crystal — one that does not go out when it grows cold.

العرّافة · The Oracle

This poem begins with a tear that cannot decide whether to fall. What follows is one of the most sustained acts of surrender in the Arabic language — not the surrender of defeat but the surrender of someone who has tried control and found it useless. The speaker stops directing. He lets the tear go where it wants. He lets it travel, water dry earth, wet pages that have gone stiff in notebooks he no longer opens. He lets it decide.

And then the reversal. The tear does not want to decide. It has found something better to do than fall — it has taken up residence in the eyelid and grown comfortable there, doing its quiet work on the person who carries it.

The composition gives that instruction its form. Teal sweeps across linen like a tide that has not yet decided to recede. Acid green holds the lower edge, alive and unsettled. At the centre, barely visible, a moon — the traditional witness of Arabic poetry, the thing that does not go out when it grows cold. The script descends through all of it, tightening as it falls, the way a voice does when it says something it means completely.
